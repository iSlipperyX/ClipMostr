ClipMonster 3.6.0 adds **ClipMonster for Mac**: ClipMonster on a Mac, making clips by itself with Claude, Gemini or a local AI on the Mac's graphics. Link your PC whenever you like, and the Mac can also use the PC's local AI, have the PC make whole videos on its graphics card, and run the PC: watch what's waiting to post, OK it or delete it, ask for new videos and keep an eye on automation, over an encrypted connection that only Macs you link can use.

## New
- **ClipMonster for Mac.** Download `ClipMonster-mac.dmg` (Apple silicon) or `ClipMonster-mac-intel.dmg` (Intel Mac) below and drag **ClipMonster** to Applications (macOS 14 Sonoma or newer).
  - **The Studio and the Library, on the Mac.** Paste YouTube links, playlists or channels, or drop videos (on the window or the Dock icon). The clips it finds show on the timeline to fix, and export with captions, as on the PC. Everything it runs is inside the app: ffmpeg with the Mac's media engine (VideoToolbox), Deno for YouTube, and Whisper and the local AI on the Mac's graphics (Metal) on Apple silicon.
  - **Its own AI.** The first start asks: Claude, Gemini, the local AI on this Mac (a model picked for its memory, from Llama 3.2 3B on an 8 GB Mac to Qwen2.5 32B on 36 GB or more), your PC's local AI, or no AI. API keys stay in the macOS Keychain.
  - **Mac ways.** Mac menus and shortcuts (⌘O, ⌘V, ⌘E, ⌘1 to ⌘9, ⌘,), notifications, a menu bar icon, dark mode following the Mac, and closing the window keeps it running until you quit.
- **Link your PC, and the Mac gets more.** On the PC, open **Settings → Mac app**, tick **Let my Mac run ClipMonster**, Save, and press **Link a Mac**: it shows the PC's IP address, port and an authorization code. On the Mac, open **Your PC → Link your PC**, type the three and press **Link**. PCs on the same network are also listed there, with their address and port filled in for you. Then:
  - **Make on: Your PC.** Beside the paste box: the PC downloads the video (or gets the file from the Mac), finds the clips and exports them with the Mac's settings on its graphics card, and the clips come back to the Mac's Library. If the PC can't be reached, the Mac offers to make it itself.
  - **Your PC's local AI** as one of the Mac's AIs: the PC's model picks the clips, so a big model works from a small Mac. Only its local AI answers the Mac, never the PC's Claude or Gemini key.
  - **Overview**: what automation is doing right now, with the same step bar as the Automation tab. Also: videos waiting for your OK, the queue, today's posts, free disk space, what's up next, each TikTok account and the news (posts, milestones, problems).
  - **Waiting to post**: every video with its picture, held ones first. Watch it as it streams from the PC, then **OK to post**, **Post now**, **Try again** or **Delete** (asks first; cleared like Clean up does). You can edit its caption and hashtags before it posts.
  - **New video** (make a Creator video, clip links, ask for clips), **Requests** (with Cancel), **Posted** (views, likes, comments, shares), **Categories** (pause, resume, find videos now), **This week** and the **Log**.
  - **Pause** automation from the Mac. The Dock and menu bar icons count the videos waiting for your OK, and Mac notifications say when one waits, when a post goes up and when something needs you.
- **Sports clips, sent to your Mac.** Give a category a **Sport** (Categories tab) and its videos are clipped as sports: the crowd erupting, the commentator's big calls and the replays find the plays, and each clip is one whole play, from a few seconds of build-up through the moment to the reaction, ending before the replay. Ten sports have their own commentary words and clip lengths (soccer, basketball, American football, baseball, ice hockey, tennis, boxing/MMA, golf, cricket, motorsport), plus *Any sport*. Every new clip also lands in the **Sports clips** inbox of ClipMonster for Mac, with a notification: watch it, save it, or let it save itself into `~/Movies/ClipMonster`. Any category can send its clips to your Macs (**Send new clips to my Macs**).
- **The Sports tab: pick the games, get the best plays of each.** Set the sport, league, teams, how recent, which footage (full games, extended highlights, highlights) and channels, then **Find games**: uploads are read into games (reactions, previews and video games left out) and uploads of the same game are shown as one. Tick the games you want and **Find highlights**: each game is listened to for the crowd and the commentary, and the AI (Claude or Gemini with your key, or the local AI on your PC) rates every play, names it, writes a title, names the players and drops replays of the same play. The best are ticked: **Make clips** for a category, or **Open in Studio** to fix them first. Automation does the work while it runs. The same tab is in ClipMonster for Mac under *Your PC → Sports*, with the PC doing the work.
- **Settings → Mac app** on the PC: turn the server on, link a Mac with the IP address, port and authorization code it shows, see the linked Macs (last seen, and from where) and unlink them.

- **ClipMonster on a Linux server, such as a Google Cloud VM.** One command installs it on Ubuntu or Debian: `curl -fsSL https://github.com/iSlipperyX/ClipMostr/releases/latest/download/install-linux.sh | sudo bash`. It runs automation, the Mac app's server and the Telegram bot as services that start with the server. You run it from your Mac. `sudo clipmonster link-mac` shows the IP address, port and authorization code to type there. `sudo clipmonster desktop` shows ClipMonster's own window in your web browser, through an SSH tunnel, for Settings and the TikTok and YouTube sign-ins. `sudo clipmonster firewall` prints the Google Cloud firewall rule the Mac needs. Running the command again, or `sudo clipmonster update`, updates it. See *Linux host (Google Cloud)* in the README.

## Security
- The server is off until you turn it on, and it runs only while automation runs. Each connection is encrypted with the PC's own certificate, which the Mac pins after linking.
- Linking proves both computers saw that same certificate, so another computer on your Wi-Fi can't pose as the PC or the Mac. The code is checked through 200,000 rounds of PBKDF2 and is thrown away after five wrong tries.
- The Mac keeps its key in the macOS Keychain. The PC keeps only a hash of it. Wrong keys from one address are slowed down.
- Nothing is opened to the internet. To use it away from home, put Tailscale on both computers and type the PC's Tailscale address as its IP address in the Mac app.

## Good to know
- The first time automation starts the server, Windows asks whether ClipMonster may use the network. Allow it on **private** networks.
- ClipMonster for Mac isn't signed with an Apple developer ID, so macOS blocks it the first time. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway** (on macOS 14, right-click → **Open** works too).
- Linking a PC needs ClipMonster 3.6.0 or newer on it. ClipMonster for Mac takes over from the dev builds called ClipMonster Remote: it brings their linked PCs over, so there's nothing to link again. macOS may ask once whether ClipMonster may use the old app's key in your Keychain: press **Always Allow**. Then delete ClipMonster Remote.
- League and broadcaster footage is usually claimed on TikTok (copyright) and can be muted or taken down. Channels that allow reuse, or your own footage, are safest for a sports account.
- The PC keeps each clip it sends to the Macs for a week, or until every linked Mac has it, in `%APPDATA%\ClipMonster\deliveries` (hard links, so no extra space while the clip is also in its folder).
- On a cloud server, YouTube asks to "confirm you're not a bot" more often than at home. Signing ClipMonster in to YouTube usually gets past it. TikTok can also give posts from a data-centre address less reach, so watch the first week before moving every account.

## Command line
- `ytclip remote status | link | on | off | unlink`: the Mac app's server without the window. `link` shows the IP address, port and authorization code, and waits for the Mac.
- `ytclip sports-montage SOURCE --sport SPORT`: a recap reel of a game's best plays (see *Recap reels* in the README).
- `ytclip auto` runs the Mac app's server too, like automation started from the app.
- `ytclip self-test` also checks the Mac app's secure connection.
