ClipMonster 3.10.0 turns the Telegram bot into an **app inside Telegram**: watch the videos waiting to post, say what's wrong with one, and the AI makes it again. **Two people** can share the bot, and each sees what the other said and did, by name.

## New
- **The Mini App.** In your bot's chat, the **ClipMonster** button left of the message box opens ClipMonster as an app: the videos to review, the queue, the library and what's posted. Tap one to watch it full size, **OK to post**, **Post now** or **Delete**, and step to the next. It follows your Telegram theme, light or dark. With the app up, the bot's messages carry a button into it instead of lists and buttons, and videos are watched in the app rather than uploaded into the chat.
- **Say what to fix, and the AI makes it again.** Under a video the Creator or the Studio made, write what should be different in your own words ("the hook is flat, open with the twist", "the third picture doesn't fit what's said"). The AI gets the video's storyboard and your critique, rewrites what you named and keeps the rest; the video is made again from that. The new version **always waits for an OK** beside the first one, each showing what was said and by whom, and the first is held meanwhile so it doesn't post as it is. If it can't be made again, the first version goes back the way it was and you're told why. A Studio video is made again in the same shape. Clips of someone else's video can't be rewritten: delete the ones that are no good. Without the app, **🛠 Say what to fix** under a video in the chat does the same.
- **Two people share the bot.** The person linked first invites one more: **People → Make an invite** in the app (or `/people` in the chat) gives a link that works once, for 10 minutes. Both see every video. Every OK, delete, post, critique, request, pause and resume carries the name of who did it: the other person gets a quiet line in the chat, and **Activity** in the app lists it all, for everyone or one person. What someone asks for comes back to them and is shown to the other. The first person can remove the second; either can leave. Two is the limit: a third chat is told the bot is full, and the code isn't used up.
- **Ask for a video from the app.** *Make*: the idea, the category, and it's in automation's queue with your name on it.

## How the app reaches your phone
- Telegram only opens an `https://` address a phone can reach, and a PC at home has none. By default ClipMonster runs Cloudflare's free **cloudflared** (downloaded once, some tens of MB, from Cloudflare's releases on GitHub) for a temporary address. Nothing is opened in your router or firewall, and no account is needed.
- The temporary address is **new each time automation starts**. The ClipMonster button in the chat is pointed at it again each time; a button in an older message stops working after a restart. Cloudflare makes no promise that these temporary addresses stay up: when one drops, ClipMonster starts another.
- For an address that stays, put your own in *Settings → Telegram → Mini App address* (a named Cloudflare tunnel, Tailscale Funnel or a reverse proxy, leading to this PC's Mini App port, 47330 unless you change it).
- **Who gets in.** The address alone opens nothing. Telegram signs who is opening the app with your bot's token; ClipMonster checks that signature and only answers the people linked to the bot. Someone removed is out at once. Videos you watch travel through Cloudflare (or your own address) to your phone, encrypted on the way.
- *Settings → Telegram → Mini App* turns it off; the bot then works by messages and buttons in the chat, as before. If the app has no address (no internet for cloudflared, say), the chat does that by itself and `/app` says why.

## Better
- **Settings → Telegram** shows who shares the bot, whether the Mini App is up, and won't link a third phone.
- A video one person asked for from the phone is shown to both when it's made.
- Automation's log says who did what from Telegram.

## Good to know
- The Mini App needs automation running on the PC, like the bot.
- A critique is read by the AI that writes your scripts (*Settings → Creator → Script writer*): this PC's own model, or the cloud one you chose, in which case the storyboard and the critique are sent to it.
- A telegram.json from before this version with more than two linked phones keeps the two linked first.
