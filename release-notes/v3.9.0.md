ClipMonster 3.9.0 adds the **Studio**: a video from a prompt *or a public article's address*, upright or wide, from a few seconds to 20 minutes, laid out as a **timeline you can change and render again**. It's opt-in: the Video Creator you know is still the default until you switch.

## New
- **The Studio.** In the **Creator** tab set *Made with* to **Studio (an editable timeline)**, or paste an article's address, which switches to it. On the command line it's `ytclip studio`. It runs as eight steps shown as it goes (input, script, voice, pictures, timeline, render, check, deliver) and when it stops it says in which. The finished video joins its category's posting queue like any Creator video, with the same AI-generated label, fact check, originality check and review before posting.
- **A video from an article.** Give a public page's address and the Studio reads it once and retells it in **new words**, written to 130–155 words a minute for the length you asked for. The script is compared with the article: wording lifted from it is asked for again, and a script that keeps copying is refused. The article's address, title, site, author and date are kept as the video's first source in its README, `clips.json`, `storyboard.json` and `timeline.json`, so you can credit it when you post. Only pages anyone can open are read: nothing behind a sign-in or a paywall (a page that marks itself paid-for isn't read even when its text is in it), the site's `robots.txt` is asked first and respected, also wherever the address redirects, and addresses on your own network are refused.
- **Upright or wide, short or long.** *Shape* in the Creator tab (or `--format short|long`, `--orientation vertical|landscape|square`): 9:16 for TikTok, Shorts and Reels, 16:9 for YouTube. A long video is planned as chapters first, then written a chapter at a time, with a chapter list in its README. Creator Rewards' 62–85 s window still applies to an upright video of about a minute; a wide or long one is the length you asked for.
- **Stock footage** from Pexels and Pixabay, through their official APIs with **your own free keys** (*Settings → Creator*). Each scene gets, in order: a clip or picture from your B-roll folder, stock footage, a picture drawn on this PC, a built-in texture. `--visuals stock` puts stock first. Only a scene's few search words are sent; answers are kept for a day; every file's author, page and licence go into the README and the timeline.
- **Kinetic captions.** One to three words at a time inside the safe zone (clear of TikTok's buttons and caption), popping in with the spoken word lit. Money words turn green, warnings red, questions yellow, the script's emphasis words the style's second colour. *Captions* in the Creator tab, or `--captions pop|bounce|karaoke|plain|off`.
- **One render, one mix.** The whole timeline is drawn, mixed and encoded in a single ffmpeg run, so nothing is encoded twice. The voice is set to −14 LUFS, the music is ducked about 16 dB under speech by a sidechain compressor and comes back up in the pauses, whooshes land on the cuts and a riser leads into the payoff, and the mix is mastered to −14 LUFS with peaks under −1 dBTP.
- **The timeline is yours to edit.** Each Studio video's folder has `timeline.json` beside `scenes/` and `assets/`: every clip with its start, length, keyframes and transition, every sound with its level and fades, every caption word with its time and colour. Change it, then **Render timeline again** in the Creator tab (or `ytclip studio --timeline <folder>`) makes `<title> (edit).mp4`; a misspelt key is said, with the nearest real name. The edit is listed with its video: where posting watches the folder it joins the queue labelled AI-generated and **always waits for your OK** (remove the one you don't want posted). Render again and the edit is replaced; one saved elsewhere with `--out` isn't listed, and the render says what posting will make of it. The Creator tab draws the timeline under the storyboard: hover a block to see what it is and who to credit.
- **ElevenLabs voices, if you choose them.** *Settings → Creator → Voice engine → ElevenLabs* (or `--tts elevenlabs`) with your own key. The script's text is sent to ElevenLabs and it costs money a character, so it's never used unless you pick it. The local voices (Kokoro, Piper, Windows' own, eSpeak NG) stay the default.

## Better
- **Pictures in the video's shape.** The picture models and the built-in textures draw wide pictures for a wide video instead of cropping upright ones.
- **Clean up** removes a posted Creator or Studio video's storyboard, scene pictures, timeline and sound files along with the video, once there's nothing left to post from its folder (an edit still waiting counts). Its record of what was posted is kept as before.
- **Posting's intake** never takes a Studio video's stock clips or other makings as videos to post, and a video sitting unlisted beside a Creator video (or in a folder inside its folder) isn't queued on its own: add it by hand.
- **The classic Video Creator** says so when a script or storyboard is longer than one video takes (20 scenes) instead of dropping the rest silently, and fits an ElevenLabs narration to the length instead of paying for a second reading.
- **Captions** (clips too): words that fit on two lines are always two lines; evening the lines out could make three.

## Good to know
- **What the Studio doesn't do.** It doesn't hide from TikTok that the posting browser is automated, and it doesn't alter videos to get past copyright or content matching. There are no talking-head avatars in this version: they come with a consent question (only your own face, or a generated one) and model licences to settle first. Pictures aren't sent to paid cloud generators (Replicate, Fal).
- **Long videos need memory to render:** about 40 MB a scene at 1080p, so a ten-minute video with 150 scenes wants around 6 GB free. The Studio warns before it starts when there isn't that much. Windows also limits how long one command may be, so a timeline with more video clips than fit (several hundred) is refused in plain words; stills don't count. Rendering a very long timeline in sections is planned.
- **Credit what you use.** Pexels' and Pixabay's licences allow their footage in your own videos, including ones that earn money, but not selling the footage on its own or suggesting the people in it endorse something. Facts in an article aren't anyone's property, but the writer's words are, which is why the script retells rather than copies; you're still responsible for what you publish.
- Better article reading is optional from source: `pip install -e ".[studio]"`. The release build reads articles with its own reader.

## Command line
- `ytclip studio "Why the Roman Empire really fell" --category History`
- `ytclip studio --url https://example.org/a-public-article --seconds 60 --captions karaoke`
- `ytclip studio "How lighthouses worked before electricity" --format long --seconds 480 --no-post`
- `ytclip studio "…" --storyboard-only --out storyboard.json`, then `ytclip studio --storyboard storyboard.json`
- `ytclip studio --timeline "path\to\video folder"`, `ytclip studio --check`, `ytclip studio --schema`
