ClipMonster 2.4.2 adds a YouTube sign-in of ClipMonster's own, so YouTube rarely asks the PC to "confirm you're not a bot" and automation stops pausing for it.

## New
- **Sign in to YouTube.** A new button in the Automation tab (and in Settings → Download) opens Firefox with a profile kept just for ClipMonster, separate from any browser you use. Sign in to YouTube there once, then close that Firefox window. From then on every download, and automation, is signed in. Signed-in downloads are what keep YouTube from asking this PC to sign in, and they also open age-restricted and members-only videos.
  - The window follows along: it says when you're signed in, when to close Firefox, and how long the sign-in lasts.
  - Signing in ends a sign-in pause straight away, and videos waiting for it carry on.
  - Use a spare Google account, not your main one. ClipMonster never sees or saves the password. It reads the sign-in cookies from that Firefox profile, in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\ClipMonster\youtube-login`.
  - Don't use that Firefox for anything else. A sign-in lasts longest when nothing else touches it. If YouTube ever asks again, sign in again from the same button. **Forget this sign-in** deletes it.
  - Needs Firefox. If it isn't installed, the window offers to get it.
- **`ytclip sign-in`** does the same from the command line. `ytclip sign-in --status` says whether it's signed in, and `ytclip sign-in --forget` removes the sign-in.
- **The health check says whether downloads are signed in**, and until when.

## Changed
- When YouTube asks the PC to sign in, the log and the Automation tab now point to **Sign in to YouTube**. If ClipMonster's sign-in was already in use, they say it may have run out and how to sign in again.
- Settings → Download → Use browser login has a new first choice, **ClipMonster's own sign-in (Firefox)**. Signing in picks it for you. Your own Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Brave still work as before.

## Updating
Installed copies offer this update automatically. It installs with the new installer from 2.4.1.
